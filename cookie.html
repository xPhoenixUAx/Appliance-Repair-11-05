2. What Are Cookies?

Cookies are small text files placed on your computer or mobile device when you visit a website. Cookies are widely used to make websites work efficiently, remember preferences, improve user experience, and provide information to website operators.

Cookies can be session cookies that expire when you close your browser, persistent cookies that remain for a set period or until deleted, first-party cookies set by the website you are visiting, or third-party cookies set by another domain.

3. Types of Cookies We Use

3.1 Essential Cookies

These cookies are necessary for the website to function properly. They may support security, network management, accessibility, form functionality, spam prevention, and basic site operations. You cannot opt out of cookies that are strictly necessary for the website to work.

  • Cookie consent: remembers cookie preferences where a consent tool is enabled.
  • Session management: maintains basic browsing or form session functionality.

3.2 Analytics Cookies

These cookies help us understand how visitors interact with our website by collecting and reporting information such as page views, traffic sources, device type, rough location, and interaction patterns. This helps us improve website performance, usability, and service request quality.

  • Analytics tools: may track website traffic, page views, and user behavior.

3.3 Functional Cookies

These cookies enable enhanced functionality and personalization. They may be set by us or by third-party providers whose tools are added to the website.

  • Form data: may temporarily store information entered into contact or quote request forms.
  • Service request flow: may help preserve appliance category or symptom selections during a browsing session.

3.4 Marketing and Call Tracking Cookies

These cookies may be used to understand how visitors arrive at the website, measure advertising performance, support call tracking, and improve lead routing for appliance repair requests. They may help determine which marketing sources produce calls, quote requests, or service inquiries.

  • Advertising networks: may track ad performance and engagement.

  • Social media tools: may enable sharing, retargeting, or campaign measurement.

5. How to Manage Cookies

You have the ability to accept, reject, block, or delete cookies. Most web browsers automatically accept cookies, but you can usually modify browser settings to decline cookies or alert you when cookies are being used.

5.1 Browser Settings

You can control cookies through browser settings in Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Safari, Microsoft Edge, and other browsers. Please refer to your browser's help documentation for current instructions.

5.2 Opt-Out Tools

You may also be able to opt out of certain third-party analytics or advertising cookies through tools provided by Google Analytics, the Digital Advertising Alliance, the Network Advertising Initiative, or similar industry resources.

Blocking or deleting cookies may affect your experience on our website. Some features, forms, tracking preferences, or request flows may not function properly if cookies are disabled.

6. Do Not Track Signals

Some browsers include a "Do Not Track" (DNT) feature. Because there is no uniform standard for how DNT signals should be interpreted, our website does not currently respond to DNT browser signals or mechanisms.
